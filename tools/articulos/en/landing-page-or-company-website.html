When a business decides to invest in its online presence, the same question tends to come up: does it need a company website, a landing page, or both? They look alike —both are web pages, both are designed and published— but they are built for different jobs. Confusing them is one of the most common ways to spend money without getting results.

What a company website is

The company website is the business’s home on the internet. It is where someone lands after hearing about you, seeing your name on a card, in a recommendation or on Google, and wanting to check whether you are worth calling.

That is why it has to explain the whole business: what you do, for whom, how you work and how to get in touch. It usually has several sections or pages, a menu to move between them and a long life: it is built to last for years and is updated along the way.

Its job is to build trust. A website that is slow, looks wrong on a phone or does not make clear what the company does means that call never happens.

What a landing page is

A landing page is a single page with a single goal: getting the visitor to do one specific thing. Request a quote, book an appointment, leave a phone number or buy. Nothing else.

To achieve that, it removes everything that distracts, including the menu. It does not try to tell the company’s story or show every service: it presents an offer, explains why it matters and asks for an action. Anything that does not help that action happen is left out.

The most expensive mistake: sending advertising to the homepage

It is very common to pay for ads on Google or social media and send whoever clicks to the website’s homepage. The visitor arrives looking for something specific —what the ad promised— and finds a general page, with a menu full of options and a message that is not the one that brought them there. Many leave without doing anything.

Every click in a campaign is paid for. That is why it makes sense for it to land on a page made for that campaign, with the same message as the ad and a single possible action. The company website is not badly built: it is simply not designed for that job.

When you need each one

A company website, if…

  • Your business does not have a website yet, or the one it has is outdated or does not work well on a phone.
  • Your clients look you up by name before hiring you and need to find something serious.
  • You want to appear on Google when someone searches for your service in your area.

A landing page, if…

  • You are going to invest in advertising and want that budget to turn into enquiries, not just visits.
  • Your business depends on a steady flow of quote requests, appointments or bookings.
  • You have a launch, a promotion or an event with a start and an end date.

The usual answer: both, each in its place

In most cases the answer is not to choose one or the other, but to use each for what it is good at. The company website is the permanent base: the one that builds trust and the one Google ranks over time. Landing pages are created for each specific campaign and receive the traffic you pay for.

If you are not sure what suits you, start with a single question:

What needs to happen when someone lands on your page?

If the answer is “that they get to know the company”, you need a company website. If it is “that they do one specific thing now”, you need a landing page.
